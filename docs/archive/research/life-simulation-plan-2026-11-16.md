# Life simulation plan: Common Ground

**Status:** proposal, 2026-11-16. No implementation is implied. Common Ground is an original, single-player household simulation about sharing a compact home, balancing personal ambitions, and hosting a neighborhood gathering. Its distinctive tension is shared space and negotiated time: a single kitchen, bathroom, budget, and work calendar make each resident's decisions matter to the others. It takes inspiration from the life-simulation genre without copying another game's characters, terminology, interface, artwork, music, or proprietary data.

## Product and release scope

Target a complete standalone experience of comparable breadth to Cinder Vault, not a claim of equivalent production scale. One furnished, editable single-floor lot houses **three playable adults** with different jobs, starting relationships, needs and ambitions. Run a seven-day community-gathering arc with an optional achievement and continue in open-ended sandbox afterward. The player controls all three, can switch at any time, issue queued instructions or let bounded autonomy handle basic care. A playable session must produce stories through resource, schedule, relationship and spatial interactions; bars alone are insufficient.

At launch: one original 12×10 lot with three defined rooms and a yard, three authored residents, two neighboring visitors, three career tracks, six furnishings categories and 18 distinct buyable objects, eight interactions, three household projects, and one gathering with three success tiers. Include three difficulty/economy presets and a concise tutorial. No aging, children, romance system, multistory construction, procedural town, online features or character creator in initial release; focus depth on the three-person household. Counts are minimum content targets, not independent systems to invent after implementation.

## Rules and player-facing loop

### Time and decisions

Use a fixed 1/60-second host tick. One simulation minute is one real second at 1×; 2× and 4× process two or four **whole ordered minute steps** per real second, respectively. The in-game pause stops minute advancement but permits commands, construction and saving while the host remains playing. The host pause also rejects ingress. Stalls follow the host's existing dropped-time behavior, never wall-clock catch-up. Time speed, current day and next commitments are always visible. A minute is the authoritative unit: snapshots, path steps and deadlines are integer minutes; animation is cosmetic.

Each resident has a maximum four-entry intent queue. A click/keyboard command specifies resident, verb and target; the authority validates at execution, not merely on selection. Explicit player orders outrank autonomy; a replace command cancels the current interruptible activity and releases reservations. Work departure and a safety-critical exhausted rest can interrupt only according to visible priority rules. Show queued action, next scheduled commitment, blocked reason and cancel control. Failed/stale actions produce a reason and cost no money or needs. The system never teleports actors to bypass an obstruction.

### Needs and action economy

Each resident owns bounded 0–100 **energy, nourishment, comfort and connection**. Initial tuning: each minute energy −0.08, nourishment −0.10, comfort −0.06, connection −0.04; every value clamps explicitly at bounds before submitting a pool transaction. At zero, no death or automatic loss: exhaustion slows movement and blocks nonessential work; hunger and discomfort reduce work quality; loneliness reduces social response. Recovery actions have duration and visible predicted effects. Sleep at a bed (60 min, +30 energy), prepare/eat at a stove or table (30 min, +30 nourishment, costs 5 household credits), wash at a sink (20 min, +24 comfort), and chat with an available person (20 min, +15 connection each, relationship effect). Partial activities apply no completion reward, and their cost is charged at start only after legal reservation; cancellation before completion consumes elapsed time and the stated nonrefundable cost. Display this before confirmation. Initial values and tuning remain data-driven and must make a normal workday survivable without constant micromanagement.

### Household, schedules and relationships

Residents are Mira, a meticulous repairer; Sol, a community cook; and Ren, a freelance illustrator. They have distinct recurring work windows, strengths and starting relationship scores, but no hidden personality simulation. Their daily timetable shows work, selected chores and upcoming gathering commitments. Normal autonomy considers urgent needs, then scheduled tasks, then one low-cost leisure action; stable resident and action-ID tie breaks avoid oscillation. Players can opt an individual out of autonomy. Autonomy never purchases objects or spends more than the disclosed meal fee.

Relationships are symmetric pair scores from −100 to 100, plus a short recent-interaction cooldown. A chat raises a score by 4; a shared meal raises it by 6; a neglected agreed chore lowers it by 4 once per missed assignment. A score at least 20 unlocks a collaborative gathering preparation; below −20 a request can be declined with an explanation. A reconcile conversation requires both present and available, lasts 30 minutes, and raises a low score by 10. Relationship changes are visible in a recent-events ledger. Visitors follow simple scheduled arrival/departure and use the same spatial and social availability rules; they do not autonomously buy or take jobs.

### Work, economy and recovery

Three tracks: repair, kitchen and illustration. Each has a scheduled four-hour shift, base pay of 70 credits and three ranks. The adult leaves through the lot exit; work happens off-lot, their availability is shown, and they return at the exit at shift end. Arriving within 30 minutes earns full pay; arriving later earns half; missing the shift yields zero. Low energy or nourishment at departure reduces pay by 20%, shown before departure. Completed shifts award career XP; promotion grants a 10-credit base-pay increase per rank. A schedule conflict is a player decision rather than an invisible roll. A weekly 100-credit bill is due on day 7; insufficient funds create debt and a clearly visible 10-credit next-day fee, with no eviction or game-over spiral. Sell eligible furnishings at 50% purchase cost and take an extra shift to recover.

Money is one household resource pool; wages, meals, furniture and bills use bounded atomic transactions. A transaction may not cross pools, and provider action execution does not atomically cover game-owned state. The game must validate all local constraints first, perform the one applicable money transaction, then commit only operations guaranteed not to fail; use explicit reservation/release and deterministic conflict arbitration. No effects may be applied after a rejected transaction. Need gains are capped by calculating actual accepted gain, not by assuming provider clamping.

### Housing and furnishing

Fixed walls, doors and room layout; furnish through a grid-based buy/move/rotate/sell mode that works while in-game paused. Six categories: sleep, cooking, hygiene, seating, work and decor; three objects each, with cost, footprint, allowed rotations, access cells, usable actions and comfort or project tags. Objects have stable IDs, non-overlapping footprints and an access cell; validation preserves a walkable route from each resident to exit and to at least one usable bed, stove and sink. Preview shows total cost, footprint, access and actionable rejection. Occupied or reserved furniture cannot be moved/sold until its action ends or is cancelled. Move/rotate has no charge; purchases debit once on commit, sales credit once. Guarantee an exit is usable even during simultaneous activities; a single-person object reserves one interaction slot, with deterministic FIFO wait and explicit cancellation. Two-person chat reserves both actors and adjacent access positions; no overlapping occupancy, swaps or deadlocks. Replan deterministically on layout change; after a bounded wait fail visibly and release reservations.

### Goals and content budget

Three optional projects provide different routes to a day-7 gathering: **Welcome Table** (one table and two prepared meals), **Repair Circle** (two repair shifts and a workbench), and **Shared Story** (three positive conversations across at least two household pairs). Complete any two and pay a 30-credit hosting cost to invite both neighbors during the gathering window. Tier 1: host one visitor; tier 2: host both and complete two projects; tier 3: additionally achieve average household relationship ≥20 and no overdue bill. Hosting must actually schedule arrivals, let residents greet and share a meal, and record a result only after the event window. A missed gathering produces a recap and sandbox continues; retry can be scheduled the following week. Goals are optional and never terminate the sandbox.

Concrete budget: three playable residents and two visitors; 18 object definitions across six categories; eight verbs (sleep, eat, wash, chat, reconcile, work, prepare gathering, greet); three jobs × three ranks; three projects and three recap tiers; one lot and three authored economy presets; at least 12 short original event/feedback lines. Interactions such as sharing a meal are coordinated variants of these verbs, not decorative counters. One accessible text tutorial covers switching, queueing, pause, furnishing and hosting. Theme assets must be original or licensed with recorded provenance; primitive geometry and generated colors are acceptable for v1 if visually legible.

## Architecture and integration

New independent family, with no shooter-family dependency:

| Component | Proposed authority and responsibility |
|---|---|
| `src/sdk/contracts/life.ts` | `Life` service: immutable detached state/revision; read-only definitions; `enqueue(unknown)` for bounded semantic commands; explicit rejection/result feedback; content registry and types. No DOM, Three.js or game policy in kernel. |
| `common/game` | Sole authoritative minute simulation, queue, schedule/needs policy, social effects, work, occupancy, reservations, layout transactions, goals and save orchestration. Provides `Life`, registers `life-household` loader and free-pointer `ExperienceRole`. |
| `common/presentation` | Optional `Ui` and `Render` consumer; scoped orthographic Three.js diorama and DOM panels. Reads snapshots and issues commands only. `render` capability permits `src/sdk/render.ts`; owns camera lease, meshes and input contexts. |
| `common/content` | Validated resident, lot, object, career, project and gathering definitions using stable original IDs. Data contains no executable code. |
| `common/household` preset | Enables all concrete providers and content; selects `sdk/experience: common/household`, the mechanics roles below, and the navigation provider. Add catalog registration and validate complete graph. |

Use `requiresRoles` and `roleDependencies` for owner-bound resource, clock, action, objective and progression factories resolved during activation. Navigation is a pure registration-time role and needs selection, not owner activation ordering. Enable and select actual provider IDs explicitly; include the kit loader only if the final data composition actually uses it. If presentation depends on a session service, declare an explicit `common/game` dependency. The SDK extension is additive; assess SDK versioning if an existing contract must change incompatibly. Staging validates IDs and cross-references, unique resident/object IDs, finite values, bounds, map connectivity, initial object placement, action and career references, schedules, prerequisite cycles and goal reachability; report source and field paths rather than throwing for bad content. Bound lot size, population, object count, queue size, save size and search depth.

### Ordered simulation and ownership

One `control` system drains a bounded snapshot batch, then advances zero or more whole minute steps based on the fixed-tick accumulator and selected speed. In each minute: (1) process ordered player changes, (2) advance explicit game clock, (3) resolve due completions and release reservations, (4) apply clamped decay, (5) process shift/bill/gathering deadlines, (6) select autonomy and validate intents, (7) arbitrate reservations and move one cell, (8) record committed goals and result, (9) publish revision/events. At identical times, completions occur before new commitments; an action finishing at shift start can depart that minute. Stable sort by resident ID and then intent sequence; ordered cardinal neighbors break path ties. Document exact departure and event-window inclusivity in implementation tests. Faster speeds repeat this entire order; never batch-decay past events.

`createCommandQueue` owns ingress, rejects host-paused commands and clears on observed pause; acceptance does not mean success. In-game pause is distinct from `SessionControl.playing`. Queue player commands separately from per-resident plans, normalize detached values and cap both. No unbounded loops or frame-time decisions. Use a consumer-owned seconds-domain `MechanicClock` advanced by **one second per simulated minute**, an explicit mapping; use `createDeadline` for activities and one-shot timing where applicable. Schedule recurrence is family-owned integer calendar policy, not a second hidden clock. Needs and money pools are separate consumer-owned handles; career ledgers and objectives are separately owned as needed. All handlers, input contexts, UI, roots, GPU resources and handles belong to `ctx`; no module-scope mutable state, wall clock, DOM in simulation, or `Math.random`. Avoid gameplay RNG for v1; if later introduced use `ctx.rng` and solve resumable RNG state before saving. Visual interpolation and cosmetic RNG cannot affect authority. Clean reservations, pending commands and handles on restart/dispose; guarded asynchronous work cannot attach to dead scopes.

## Persistence design and critical feasibility gate

Save is necessary for a seven-day household. Use a bounded versioned **deterministic replay checkpoint** under `common/save/<household-id>` through `Profile`, preserving unrelated profile values, with explicit save/load and surfaced failure. Store content identity/version, seed, initial household selection, and a bounded ordered stream of accepted semantic commands tagged with authoritative minute/tick order and speed/pause changes. Reconstruct a temporary household and replay each whole minute including autonomy, then validate final checksum and only swap state after success. Store no wall-clock delta; save while activities are in progress, with queues and all deadlines reconstructed by replay. Do not assume detached resource snapshots restore clocks, action cooldowns, objective receipts or progression. Check exact `Profile` capacity before choosing maximum replay size and frequency; cap at a documented practical limit, show remaining capacity, and reject a new save rather than truncate or corrupt. Start with a bounded 14-day replay budget and benchmark worst-case load; sandbox continues after capacity but user is informed saving requires starting a new household until checkpoint support ships. If limits or replay latency make this unacceptable, implement and test a **versioned provider checkpoint/restore contract** for every selected provider and atomic detached reconstruction before claiming unlimited sandbox saves; do not silently skip handles or invent private imports.

Load must reject malformed, oversize, incompatible content/version, illegal commands, checksum mismatch and unsupported schema without changing the live household or previous save. Saving is only at a deterministic completed-tick boundary, includes pending resident orders, and clears/transitions the ingress queue under an explicit policy. Save failures do not reset play. Since shared provider mutations reject when host paused, reconstruction must happen at a permitted playing boundary using isolated temporary owner scope, never by weakening pause or mutating the active instance during validation. Verify that actual host APIs support a detached temporary scope and that replaying providers cannot leak events into the active session; if not, this is a blocking implementation prerequisite requiring a bounded detached simulation/restore design, not an assumption. Version migration is explicit; unsupported versions report incompatibility. Test a nontrivial mid-activity round trip and continued equality after reload.

## Interface and accessibility

Present an original overhead dollhouse with visible room boundaries, resident markers and silhouettes, selected-person highlight, usable object labels and reservation/wait indicators. Desktop panels: household switcher, four labeled needs with values and trends, queue with cancel/reorder, day timeline, money/bills, relationships, goals and recent events. Buy mode exposes keyboard-accessible object list, grid movement and rotate/confirm/cancel; all pointer operations have keyboard equivalents. Tab order, semantic buttons, accessible names, visible focus, text labels plus color, status announcements for blocked actions and milestones, readable contrast, scalable text, reduced motion and optional audio are acceptance criteria. No gameplay information is only conveyed by sound, hover or color. Reflow for narrow screens with accessible tabs and an object list alternative to precise scene picking; pointer policy is free. Provide pause, speed, tutorial and reset controls without timing pressure. UI must not mutate authority while host-paused.

### Full acceptance scenarios

- **Seven-day household:** start preset, switch among all three, assign work and care, resolve a queued stove conflict, buy a table, complete Welcome Table and Shared Story, greet both arriving neighbors, host a shared meal, pay bill and earn the appropriate tier. Verify every event's time, debit/credit, social result and project receipt; continue into day 8.
- **Failure and recovery:** block a proposed access route and see an actionable rejection with no debit; miss a shift and bill, show debt and exactly one daily fee, sell an eligible object and take an extra shift, pay debt, then reschedule the gathering. No teleport, duplicate charge or silent loss.
- **Conflicting actors:** two residents request one bed, one cancels while waiting, a third moves furniture after reservation release; an unreachable or interrupted two-person chat releases both participants. Identical seed and commands yield identical snapshots and ordered committed events.
- **Schedules and speed:** action completes exactly at shift start, off-lot return conflicts with occupied exit, and a gathering window crosses at 4×. Compare the same minute-by-minute outcomes at 1× and 4×, including bounds and event precedence; pause produces no decay or overdue event until resumed.
- **Save/load:** save while a resident is mid-shift, another waiting for furniture and the third in a social action; reload in a fresh session, compare complete detached state and then advance identical commands/minutes to equal wages, reservations, needs, cooldowns, project receipts and results. Corrupt, oversize and version-mismatched saves leave live state and original save intact. Exercise the documented maximum replay budget and load latency.
- **Accessibility/lifecycle:** keyboard-only switch, queue, cancel, purchase, rotate, host and save; inspect focus, labels, announcements, scaling, narrow layout and reduced motion. Restart/switch games repeatedly and prove no residual systems, input contexts, roots, leases, listeners, handles or pending commands. Headless tests run with no UI/render service and stage-invalid content reports precise paths.
