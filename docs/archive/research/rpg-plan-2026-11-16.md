# The Salt Ledger — standalone RPG production plan

**Status: proposal, 2026-11-16.** No gameplay in this document is implemented. This is a standalone, authored, party-based tactical RPG, not another Cinder preset. Target a complete first playthrough of roughly 4–6 hours, subject to playtesting, and two meaningfully different routes. The scope is comparable to Cinder Vault's three generated depths, three classes, seven enemy archetypes, twelve items and nine recipes, but spends its budget on a persistent authored world and consequences instead of procedural runs and first-person weapons. These counts are targets, not evidence of existing content.

## Premise and player promise

The sea returns the names of the dead as salt tablets. The city of **Tern** burns those names to keep its storm barrier lit; inland communities demand their ancestors back. As a licensed **Ledger Bearer**, the player investigates why the barrier is failing and decides who controls the remaining names. The defining interaction is **Witness**: evidence gathered in exploration can change a negotiation, expose an enemy's vulnerability, or make a regional settlement possible. Evidence is an authored fact, not a random persuasion roll or a collectible currency.

The loop: visit a location, speak and inspect; choose a quest approach; enter a tactical encounter or take an authored noncombat route; earn XP and equipment; return to a hub to resolve consequences; use three regional seals to reach the storm engine. Travel and combat happen in the same campaign, and choices persist. A quest's peaceful solution must require an actual prior action, not a free skip button.

## World, characters and authored budget

Four regions, **16 discrete connected locations** total, each with its own readable description, interactions and map node. Locations are not counted again as encounters. One hub and three frontier regions; paths may be revisited after unlocking. Travel is a semantic node action, not continuous overworld locomotion. Combat boards are separate authored grids associated with locations.

| Region | Four locations | Identity and main conflict |
|---|---|---|
| Tern Harbour | Ledger Hall, Flood Market, Old Quay, Breakwater Engine | Civic hub; rationed names power the failing barrier. Old Quay is the tutorial; Engine is the finale. |
| Glass Fen | Reed Ferry, Salt Orchard, Silt Shrine, Drowned Archive | Ferrymen protect the dead from the city's collectors; the archive reveals the barrier's true cost. |
| Bellwether Ridge | Switchback, Bell Foundry, Miners' Rest, Wind Cairn | Foundry workers strike against forced tablet extraction; wind conductors could replace some fuel. |
| The White Scar | Pilgrim Gate, Ossuary Steps, Empty Basilica, Storm Mouth | A pilgrimage route across salt flats; a keeper maintains the last seal and challenges the party's claim. |

**Six named characters:** Iona Vale, the Bearer and player protagonist; **Neri**, ferrymaster and defensive companion; **Pell**, ex-collector and ranged companion; **Sable**, foundry medic and support companion; **Magistrate Orr**, Tern's pragmatic barrier administrator; **The Cantor**, keeper of the final seal. Four playable party members total, with Iona plus up to two recruited companions active; the inactive companion stays at the hub and shares campaign XP. All three companions can be recruited on one playthrough. Each gets an introductory conversation, one personal quest, and at least two changed lines keyed to a regional decision. Recruitment never becomes permanently impossible.

**Quest budget: 12 quests, exactly:** five main quests (tutorial contract, three region seals, finale), four side quests and three companion quests. The 12 are independent authored IDs, not dialogue-node counts. Each has a start, journal update, completion condition, reward and at least one explicit prerequisite or location condition. The three seal quests each have two resolutions; at least two side quests also offer a different solution with a visible consequence. Suggested side quests: recover a ferry manifest; repair a foundry vent; return a stolen name; expose a false salt assay. Companion quests: Neri's lost passenger, Pell's collection warrant, Sable's abandoned ward. Each provides a concrete evidence flag or piece of gear; none is mandatory to finish.

**Encounter budget: 10 distinct authored boards:** tutorial at Old Quay; two each in Fen, Ridge and Scar; one optional side encounter; Breakwater approach; Engine finale. Six are mandatory on the baseline combat route (tutorial, one per seal, approach, finale); regional alternatives can bypass their seal board after completing evidence work, and must be tested as separate routes. Each board has named entry/exit cells, terrain, deployment, encounter-specific composition and victory/defeat logic. No respawning enemies or procedural content are assumed. Three region boards have an environmental lever (floodgate, vent, bell) that changes cover or a hazard on a committed action.

**Enemies: eight regular archetypes plus two bosses**, not ten variants of one actor: collector (melee), harpooner (pull telegraph), salt hound (two-step flank), fen wisp (ranged mark), foundry guard (block), cinder drone (line hazard), penitent (heals ally), cantor acolyte (silence); Cantor and Engine Custodian are bosses. Intent must describe the action actually selected. Bosses have one telegraphed phase change at half health; encounter objectives and named noncombat resolution are local policy, not promises of the shared action provider.

**Content quantities:** 16 locations; 12 quests; 10 boards; 10 enemy archetypes including two bosses; 4 playable characters; 6 named characters total; 24 equipment/consumable/ingredient item definitions (8 weapons, 6 armor, 4 charms, 3 consumables, 3 ingredients); 6 known recipes using those definitions; 12 active abilities (3 per playable character); 12 perk choices (3 per character); 3 endings. A character can equip only their authored allowed weapons; there are three equipment slots per character: weapon, armor, charm. One shared bag, separate equipment handles per character: implement explicit safe transfers rather than assuming the inventory provider supports multi-inventory transactions. Alternatively, use a single party equipment slot namespace with slots per character and item slot compatibility explicitly designed up front; the current item contract has only one `slot` string per item. Resolve this incompatibility in the vertical slice; do not silently duplicate gear. Recipe costs use ingredients only, avoiding a cross-provider currency transaction.

## Play rules and example consequences

- **Combat:** orthogonal grids, deterministic turn order: player team then living enemies in stable authored ID order. Each active character has 2 AP per round; move up to 3 legal cells costs 1 AP, basic attack costs 1 AP, abilities cost their displayed AP and, where specified, focus. End turn resolves enemy intentions, advances the combat turn clock once, then restores AP. Waiting, UI and rejected actions advance neither AP nor clock. Navigation supplies unit-edge reachability; RPG code supplies occupancy, terrain, line of sight, AP conversion and commit recheck.
- **Distinct roles:** Iona strikes and marks an exposed target; Neri intercepts a hit and can push an adjacent foe; Pell shoots through a marked lane and disarms a hazard; Sable heals or cleanses. Three abilities per actor include one signature, one defensive/support action and one upgradeable tactical action. Cooldowns belong to per-actor handles. Telegraph targets and hazard cells before end turn; do not claim an enemy will do something the resolution algorithm can change without updating intent.
- **Progression:** shared party XP ledger, starting level 1, linear threshold `40 + 20 × (level − 1)`, cap 8; level rewards are one chosen perk for one character, selected from that character's three choices, at most once each. Inactive companions share levels, not combat turns. The UI blocks new combat until pending reward choices are resolved. Base stats and perk effects are game-owned. Numbers are initial tuning targets, not validated balance.
- **Loot and economy:** fixed encounter/quest rewards; inventory capacity 24 stacks; equipment and consumables change damage, defenses or actions by authored local effects. Full bags never silently destroy quest rewards: reserve rewards in a claimable location cache until fully accepted. Quest evidence is a bounded flag ledger, never a droppable inventory item, so selling ingredients cannot soft-lock the campaign. Six fixed recipes are known at the relevant hub station; crafting is ingredient-only and output failure restores inputs.
- **Recoverability:** defeat returns to the last explicit safe checkpoint, preserving only progress from that checkpoint; combat has a restart encounter action. No permadeath or time limit. Saving is permitted only at safe nodes outside combat, with a clear UI indicator. An unavailable profile means the session continues but no save is promised.

**Concrete branching:** In Glass Fen, learn the ferry manifest and confront the collector. Return names to the ferry families (Fen seal, Neri approval, Fen ally at the Engine) or send them to Orr (Fen seal, a stronger starting barrier at the Engine). Both advance the main quest, set one exclusive flag and alter dialogue and final encounter setup. At Ridge choose worker control or civic control of the wind foundry; at Scar either demonstrate the archive evidence or defeat the Cantor. The finale remains reachable from every combination. Choices lock when confirmed; the journal names their consequences before commitment.

**Finale and replay:** after the three seals, the Engine encounter changes its starting defenses and available ally action according to Fen/Ridge decisions. At the ending console choose **Keep the Barrier** (city retains names), **Return the Names** (communities regain them, city evacuates), or **Shared Covenant** (requires archive evidence and Ridge worker control; both must be signposted before the point of no return). Each ending has a distinct final scene and an epilogue line for each of the three regional choices and recruited companions. After victory the player may start a fresh campaign; choices, party and inventory reset. Replay comes from mutually exclusive decisions, optional quests, party/perk composition and the conditional ending, not random content or a claimed new-game-plus system.

## Proposed architecture and contracts

- `games/expeditions/contracts/salt-ledger.ts`: **new, proposed** `SaltLedger` service, `SaltCampaigns` registry and `SaltResolved` event; export through SDK index. A detached immutable observation contains campaign phase, location, visible exits/NPC options, party HP/AP/focus, quest/journal state, bag/equipment and pending rewards, encounter grid/intents, available commands/reasons, ending and revision. `enqueue(command): boolean` only reports queue admission; a committed/rejected result with a stable request ID and reason appears after simulation. A pure `forecast` is advisory and always rechecked at commit. Commands are discriminated IDs for travel/interact/choose, move/attack/ability/end turn, equipment/craft/perk and save/load/restart. Presentation never mutates snapshots.
- `salt/game`: single authoritative simulation and `sdk/experience` provider `salt/ledger` with free pointer. Register bounded, validated `salt-campaign` data kind and cross-reference validators; own command normalization, flags, evidence, dialogue, quests, tactical occupancy, combat, outcomes, XP/perk effects and save orchestration. Simulation has no DOM or renderer dependency. Use `createCommandQueue` with capacity 128, snapshot-batch semantics, pause and disposal gating; define request dedupe bound and rejection feedback. Each committed command runs in a fixed simulation phase; no simulation change during render.
- `salt/presentation`: consumes only `SaltLedger`, optional `Ui`, input and theme/audio. World map and keyboard-accessible grid, journal, dialogue, inventory, reward, help and save indicators. Register a HUD role only if composition requires it; use scoped input contexts and mounts. Select theme assets through semantic keys, no gameplay paths. Start with CSS/2D instead of assuming the shooter camera can operate an independent board. Use existing appropriately licensed theme assets with provenance checked, and allocate original icons/portraits as needed.
- `salt/content`: data-only campaign, locations, encounters, quests, dialogues, enemy and ability definitions, 24 shared `item` definitions and six `crafting-recipe` entries. Define non-executable typed effects/conditions with strict field limits and reference checks; no embedded script. Validate mutually exclusive flags, entry/exit nodes, quest DAG, objective references, encounter starts, spawn occupancy and all three ending routes. Static reachability and playthrough tests are separate from basic JSON validity.
- `games/expeditions/presets/salt-ledger.json` plus catalog registration: explicitly enable game, presentation, content, `mechanics/kits`, resources, clock, actions, objectives, navigation, progression, inventory, crafting and room-map, plus the selected theme. Select `sdk/experience`, theme and each mechanic role. Declare concrete content-kind dependencies, and `roleDependencies` for activation-time factories: resources, clock, actions, objectives, progression, inventory and crafting; require navigation/room-map roles and order activation where their concrete use demands it. Complete exact manifest edges against actual loader registration during implementation; no auto-enabled dependencies.

The new service is family-specific, not a new general dialogue/combat provider. If a real second consumer later requires an extraction, write the capability/evidence note and conformance cases before widening SDK. Document SDK additions, content schema, roles, systems, instructions and actual limitations in `docs/guide/plugins.md`, `docs/reference/content-format.md`, `docs/reference/architecture.md`, `docs/reference/spec.md`, `README.md` and a new game guide. Add reviewed plugin `reuse.json` and only justified recipes; run `npm run index-reuse` after source/catalog/metadata changes.

### State, transaction and save design gate

Shared provider snapshots are observations, not restore APIs. Do not serialize handles or mutate them from JSON. A versioned, campaign-ID/content-version/seed-bound checkpoint must retain every accepted state-changing semantic command from new campaign to the safe node, including deterministic combat and reward decisions; bounded log maximum and storage size must be established in the slice. Replay from a fresh isolated set of owned handles with the same deterministic random stream (prefer no gameplay randomness in v1), validate each command and final safe-node invariant, suppress external events/audio/profile writes, then atomically swap the live campaign only on full success. Save only after a successful checkpoint, not every tick; invalid/version-mismatched/truncated/over-limit data leaves current play and previous save untouched. Avoid irreversible UI/world side effects during reconstruction. If isolated replay and atomic swap cannot be implemented safely with current host ownership, the save milestone is blocked until a tested game-local restore protocol is designed; do not ship fake saves.

Composite operations require special scrutiny: provider calls are individually atomic, not globally transactional. Validate all targets and all preconditions first; use single-provider operations where possible. Equip transfer and quest reward involving multiple handles must have a provably safe game-local commit/compensation protocol, including full-bag and paused/disposed failures, or adopt a validated compatible one-inventory equipment design. Do not award quest progress before a reward is guaranteed/placed in a durable claim cache. On load/restart, dispose old handles only after a successful replacement; no duplicate event, XP or loot on replay.

## Delivery milestones and acceptance gates

1. **Contract and feasibility spike:** agree schema, deterministic command semantics, party equipment model and isolated checkpoint replay. One location, one two-unit encounter, one dialogue choice and save/load must round-trip. Prove full-bag rejection, equal-item transfers, corrupted save, restart and disposal before content production. Block on unresolved cross-provider atomicity or replay; do not substitute screenshots for behavior.
2. **Vertical slice:** Tern + one Fen board, one meaningful Fen choice, Neri recruitment, 2 abilities per represented character, inventory, one perk and one recipe. Test a combat route and evidence bypass end to end; demonstrate keyboard/pointer, headless operation and production-bundle browser play.
3. **Campaign systems:** all three frontier quest branches, travel gates, party selection, boss mechanics and save continuation. Complete normal and alternate regional paths with no combat-only mandatory item; establish numerical baseline with automated route fixtures, then playtest for balance.
4. **Content and presentation lock:** reach exact budget above, finish three distinct endings and conditional epilogues, art/theme provenance, semantic sound and help. Prove every named quest has completion, reward and journal text; all equipment and abilities are obtainable and observable in play. Track actual counts from content IDs, never count a phase, placement or palette swap as a separate archetype.
5. **Release:** `npm run index-reuse`, `npm run check`, `npm run validate-game -- --bundled`, `npm run build && npm run smoke`; add `tests/salt-demo/run.mjs` against production bundle for actual preset. Run creator demo if creator changes. Record exact commands/results and any environment failures, plus hands-on browser review and playtest findings.

**Required headless tests:** full winning route to each ending, alternative solutions for all three region seals, all quest completions and reward uniqueness, inactive companion/party limits, all encounters including two boss phase transitions and defeat recovery. Verify a proof route for every reachable flag combination and no soft-lock under spent/dropped ordinary items; test exclusive choice rejection and conditional ending gate. Validate duplicate IDs/references, cycles, unreachable gate configurations, illegal spawn/terrain, bad item slots and content errors with actionable paths; stage failure preserves existing session. Verify stale forecasts, occupied cells, range/line-of-sight, actor-specific cooldowns, turn clock and pause: rejections spend nothing; deterministic same-seed routes match state/events. Check multi-level XP and pending selection, full bag/crafting rollback, per-character gear ownership, unopened rewards and replay idempotence. Test unavailable storage, invalid/old/tampered saves, save at wrong phase, maximum log boundary, fresh-session load, no replay events, and old session preserved if load fails. Exercise provider substitution where an independent compatible test provider exists; test owner isolation, disposal and restart.

**Required browser acceptance:** launcher and preset activation, mouse and keyboard travel/dialogue, accessible focus and labeled grid, combat forecast/intent and actual turn, equip/unequip and full bag feedback, forge, pending perk selection, save/reload in a fresh browser session, defeat/retry, one full unaccelerated completion and at least one alternate choice/ending. Desktop and narrow viewport must keep controls and journal usable; no clipped mandatory action, console error or stale scene on switching games. Automation may accelerate other content routes only if documented; one representative real-combat route must run without privileged damage calls. Test audio and subjective visual clarity manually. Initial 4–6 hour target and encounter tuning remain hypotheses until timed playtests, not acceptance facts.
