# Hollowmere UI polish audit: combat feedback, HUD hierarchy, journal/map and depth

Research/access date: **2026-11-16**. Scope: the Hollowmere metroidvania presentation at
`games/hollowmere/plugins/hollowmere-presentation`, with Cinder's read-only UI as a comparison. This document proposes
changes; it does not modify runtime code.

**Evidence.** The supplied captures at `.smoke/hollowmere-*.png` and `.smoke/composer-*.png`, plus a fresh
capture of the production build at 1280×800 and 390×844. Measurements below use CSS pixels at those viewports.
A concurrent presentation pass is present in the working tree; this audit does not modify it. The supplied
screenshots are the baseline, and the recommendations below are written as the end state to verify against the
current code rather than as a claim that every item is still missing.

## Short version

Hollowmere already has a recognisable art direction: warm gold on moss, a serif journal, restrained fantasy
chrome. The presentation's problems are not a lack of decoration. They are **scale, information priority and
feedback legibility**:

1. A damage number drawn inside the camera transform becomes roughly **500 px tall**; health and focus are
   rendered at **8–11 px** and carry less weight than the room name.
2. Combat feedback is mostly one hurt frame, one number and a prose toast. There is no readable hit/kill
   hierarchy, no hitstop and no impact ring on every hit; the captured baseline communicates invulnerability
   by hiding the player at about 10 Hz.
3. The journal's content is good, but the map clips words, the legend and unexplored cells fail contrast, and
   the phone layout preserves desktop keyboard instructions while dropping map labels.
4. The world is a single painted backdrop behind a repeated 47 px grass tile. There is no foreground, no
   midground parallax, little atmospheric perspective, and several props read as debug primitives.

Fix readability and feedback first; add more particles and parallax only after the information layer works.

The rows below describe the supplied captures, not the moving working-tree state. Each recommendation is still
the end-state acceptance target regardless of what has already landed.

## 1. Floating damage numbers

### What went wrong

The captured baseline draws `fillText` **inside** the world transform:

```ts
translate(width / 2, height / 2)
scale(scale, scale)
translate(-(cameraX + viewW / 2), -(cameraY + viewH / 2))
fillText(text, effect.x, effect.y - effect.age * 1.2) // font: 11px
```

At 1280×800, one world unit is ~47 CSS px, so the label is ~518 px tall. At 390×844 portrait the scale is
`max(390/29, 844/17) = 49.65`, so it is slightly worse and the camera sees only ~7.9 world units across. The
in-progress screen-space fix is the correct direction; keep the label layer outside the camera transform and
use one projection helper for labels, slashes, projectiles and rings.

### Exact recommendation

- **Screen-space, fixed size.** Use `x = width/2 + (worldX - cameraX - viewW/2) * scale`. Size labels with a
  responsive formula, not world scale: `clamp(12, viewportHeight * 0.018, 16)` CSS px. At 800 px that is 14 px;
  at 1080 px it is 16 px. Use 12–14 px for secondary numbers, 16–20 px for heavy/critical hits (the current game
  has no crits, so a finisher/kill size tier is enough). Reject the 11 px world-space default.
- **Motion.** Spawn with a 1.12–1.2× pop over 70–90 ms, settle, rise 24–40 px with ease-out, hold full alpha
  for ~250 ms, then fade over the last 200–250 ms. Total life 0.65–0.9 s. Do not fade linearly from frame one;
  the number must be readable before it disappears.
- **Stacking and spam.** Give each target anchor slots (above, then up-left/up-right), merge hits on the same
  target within 0.45–0.6 s into one running total, and cap the visible labels at 8–12. `MetroEffectView` has no
  target id, so either add an optional `targetId` or group by hit point within ~0.6 world units and a 0.5 s
  window. Cull points outside a 24–48 px margin, and never draw labels in the top HUD safe band. Rapid combo
  hits should read as one total, not three overlapping numbers.
- **Colour and meaning.** Enemy damage: cream/gold, plain number. Player damage: red/peach with a minus sign
  (`−8`) plus an edge flash. Healing: green with `+35` and a rising, gentler motion. Do not distinguish only by
  colour: retain the sign/glyph. The current `damageEnemy` and `damagePlayer` both produce `-N`; make those
  vocabularies different.
- **Contrast.** A 3 px dark outline (`#07100b` at ~0.92) plus a 14 px font is enough over grass, sky and stone.
  Keep the label fill near `#ff9a7a` for player damage and `#f3d58a` for enemy damage. Do not add a full black
  box unless a high-contrast option is enabled.
- **Accessibility.** Keep the existing “Damage numbers” toggle; add “Reduced motion” which removes rise/pop but
  keeps a static 0.6 s label. Do **not** put every number in an `aria-live` region; announce only a coalesced
  “You take 8 damage” status or rely on the health bar's `progressbar` value. Test the label against the
  brightest region (archive/sky) and in grayscale.
- **Acceptance.** No label exceeds 16 px; max 12 active; no label under the HUD; no two labels overlap; text
  survives at 390×844 portrait and 844×390 landscape; the full combo reads as one total.

Practitioner sources agree on the hard parts: fixed screen-space size, short life, dark edge, merging rapid
hits, separating damage categories and a hard cap on simultaneous numbers [12][13][14][15].

## 2. Hit feedback and “juice”

### What the screenshots show

- In the captured baseline, the enemy's windup is only a sprite frame and the player's attack arc is a thin
  stroke. There is no progress arc, ground wedge, impact ring or white flash on a normal hit. These are the
  cues to add; a ring that is always at the same radius and same alpha is not a telegraph.
- Player damage triggers a screen shake, a toast (“The dark bites for 8.”) and a damage number. The toast
  duplicates the number and persists for 4.5 s.
- In the captured baseline, invulnerability is a full visibility blink at `floor(playTime * 20) % 2` — about 10
  visibility changes per second — so the player disappears at exactly the moment they need to read their
  position. Dimming instead of hiding is better, but a 10 Hz flicker is still harder to read than a slow pulse
  plus a rim.
- The docs call this “hitstop-like feedback”, but the game state has no hitstop field and the presentation has
  no freeze. Either implement a small hitstop contract or soften the documentation claim.

### Feedback tiers for this game

| Event | Presentation on the same frame | Optional game-side |
|---|---|---|
| Whiff | Slash arc, small dust, no shake, no number | — |
| Light hit | 80–120 ms target flash, 3 sparks, 3 px directional shake, damage label, hit sound | 40–60 ms hitstop |
| Heavy / combo finisher / kill | 1.25× label, 160–200 ms ring, 5–6 sparks, 4–6 px shake, brief squash, kill sound | 70–110 ms hitstop, cap 120 ms |
| Player hurt | 120–160 ms edge vignette, directional edge arc, 6–8 px shake, `−N` label, hurt sound | brief input-buffer-friendly stop |
| Heal | Green `+N`, soft ring, gentle rise, quiet sound | — |
| Telegraph | Progress arc/ground wedge for at least 350 ms, warm red-orange, pulse twice maximum | — |

**Hitstop.** 40–70 ms for a normal hit, 70–110 ms for a finisher or kill, never more than 120 ms. Apply it only
to the first and last hit of a combo; do not stop UI, input buffering or audio. If a game-side clock change is
too invasive for this pass, ship the flash/shake/ring layers now and either add a tiny `hitstop` field to
`MetroState` or remove the “hitstop” claim from `games/hollowmere/README.md` and `docs/reference/spec.md`. [16][17][18]

**Shake.** Use screen pixels, not world units. The captured `shake = 0.2` world units is ~9.4 px at 1280×800,
which is at the upper end of a normal hit. Budget 4–6 px on desktop, 3–4 px on phone, 150–250 ms total; shake
along the hit direction, not a two-axis sine buzz; use a decaying trauma value squared, and let only the
strongest event of a moment shake. Keep shake off during menus, dialogue and reduced-motion. [17][19]

**Flash.** Flash the **enemy**, not the whole screen, for 60–100 ms at 60–90% white/cream, then return to the
normal tint. A full-screen hit flash should be a subtle red edge only (alpha ≤0.15) and must not exceed three
flashes per second; it needs a reduce-flash replacement that still communicates damage (a low-alpha edge band
or static rim), not a silently removed warning.

**Telegraph and invulnerability.** Make the windup a progress arc plus a ground wedge, scaled to the attack
range, not just a constant ring. Keep the warm red-orange danger vocabulary, but make the fill legible in
0.5–0.7 s. Replace the 10 Hz blink with a 3–4 Hz alpha pulse at ~0.45–0.55 plus a persistent thin rim and a
small “i-frame” ring; the player must never become invisible. Slow dimming is better than disappearing, but it
is still too fast. Add a short spark/ring at i-frame start and end so the timing is learnable. Add a
hit/hurt/kill sound layer with a 3–5 simultaneous-voice cap if audio is available; sound is a cheap, high-value
feedback channel [18].

**Prose toasts.** Remove “The dark bites for N” from every hit. Reserve the bottom toast for discoveries,
saves and tutorial lines; 2–5 s depending on reading load; queue rather than overwrite. This also stops the
toast from competing with dialogue and damage labels.

## 3. HUD hierarchy, sizing and safe areas

### Findings

At 1280×800 the captured HUD has:

| Element | Captured size | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Brand `HOLLOWMERE` | 20 px | Competes with room name; decorative |
| Room name | 22 px | Strongest text on screen, although not actionable |
| `VITALITY` / `LANTERN FOCUS` | 9 px | Below the readable UI range |
| Health/focus values | 11 px | Too quiet for run-ending state |
| Bars | 8 px, 1 px border | Two identical bars, no low-health state |
| `0 / 3 SUN SIGILS` | 10 px | Objective mixed with currency |
| Controls | 9 px, two lines | Persistent, low-value once learned |
| Phone labels | 7–8 px | Unreadable outdoors / on a handheld |

The room header also repeats progress (`1 / 12 ROOMS · 9% COMPLETE`) that already appears in the journal. The
health number and the bar duplicate the same information without either becoming the anchor.

### Recommendation

**Layout.** Use three safe-area zones: brand/room top, status top-left (or a compact strip), currency top-right.
Keep the central 60% of the playfield free of persistent chrome [26][31]. Use `env(safe-area-inset-*)` (the
current styles already do) and a 5–10% title-safe margin; Xbox's 1080p title-safe area is the inner 90%
(~1720×1000) [8]. Keep the HUD under roughly 15–20% of the screen; mobile should be a
two-row strip, not three floating groups [26]. Apple's game guidance makes the same point: anchor sections to device
edges and keep controls inside safe areas [9].

**Type and hierarchy.** Suggested ramp for this aesthetic:

| Role | Desktop | Phone | Weight / colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Health number | 28–32 px | 24–28 px | serif, cream |
| Health max / label | 11–12 px | 10–11 px | mono, muted, uppercase |
| Focus number | 20–22 px | 18–20 px | serif, teal |
| Focus label | 10–11 px | 9–10 px | mono |
| Room name | 20–24 px | 16–18 px | serif, gold |
| Region / progress | 9–10 px | 8–9 px | mono, letterspaced |
| Currency / sigils | 12–14 px | 11–12 px | mono |

Do not use 7–9 px for anything a player must read. The 12 px floor is a practical minimum for UI text; 16 px is
the usual mobile body-text baseline [10][11]. Uppercase is acceptable for short labels, not descriptions;
letterspacing at 0.12–0.18em is fine for 9–11 px labels but should be reduced for longer strings.

**Health and focus.** Make health the single large number (Cinder's 42 px health number is the right instinct,
though oversized for Hollowmere), with the bar as a secondary gauge. Show focus as five discrete 10-point pips
or a segmented bar, so the 10-focus Lantern Bolt cost is visible. Give low health a static word/glyph (“LOW”)
plus a slow edge pulse; do not rely on colour alone. Use `role="progressbar"` with value/min/max on both.

**Objective and currency.** Separate “embers” from “Sun Sigils”. Embers are a currency; sigils are the run
objective. Render three named pips with `aria-label` (“Sun Sigil 1 of 3, unlit”) and use filled/unfilled shapes
in addition to the gold border. Move the room-progress percentage to the journal; the HUD only needs region,
room, health, focus, currency and objective count.

**Controls and prompts.** Fade the two-line controls block after 20–30 s, show it again on first journal open,
and keep the option to restore it. Use a contextual prompt near the interaction instead of a permanent legend.
On touch, hide keyboard hints and either provide touch controls or explicitly say “Keyboard required”; the
current 390×844 capture hides controls with no substitute. [5][26]

**Mobile reflow.** The current `max-width:560px` rule stacks the room title and turns the status into one
row; keep that direction. Verify at 390×844 and 844×390: brand, room, currency, health and focus must not
overlap or leave the safe area. The phone playfield currently shows only ~8 world units across; if phone play
is intended, fit a minimum ~16-unit width or require landscape. If it is only a journal demo, say so rather
than implying mobile gameplay support.

## 4. Journal: map legibility, tabs and interaction

### Map findings

The captured desktop map clips labels mid-word because the room name is drawn under a hard `rect` clip with no
`measureText`, ellipsis or fallback. The supplied phone capture has no labels at all because the code requires
`room.revealed && w >= 58`; at 390 px, most rooms fail that condition. A later fresh capture draws labels on
phone, but still clips them (“ssgate Thresho”, “The Long Moss”) and keeps them tiny relative to the map.

Computed contrast against the map background `#08110c` (WCAG relative luminance, alpha composited):

| Map state | Colour | Contrast | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unexplored fill | `#15221a` | **1.16:1** | Fails 3:1 for meaningful graphics |
| Unrevealed border | `#3a503c` | **2.18:1** | Fails |
| Revealed fill | `#273d31` | **1.64:1** | Fails |
| Visited fill | `#3d5b47` | **2.54:1** | Fails |
| Known edge (55% alpha) | `#53694f` | **~1.35:1** | Fails |
| Visited label | `#d4c79c` on `#3d5b47` | **4.47:1** | Just below 4.5:1 at 7–11 px |
| Current room label | `#1d2415` on `#c7b06c` | 7.49:1 | Passes |

WCAG requires 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for graphical objects needed to understand content [1][2]. The map
is exactly such a graphical object. Do not “fix” this only by brightening the whole map; use a sane three-value
ramp for states plus shape/pattern cues.

### Map recommendation

- **Label strategy.** Set a minimum label size of 11–12 CSS px and compute it in screen pixels, not from
  `scale`. Measure the room rect; if the full name does not fit, draw an ellipsis and expose the full name in a
  selected-room detail strip below the map (name, region, visited/revealed, exits, checkpoint, sigils). Never
  clip a word with a hard rect. On phone, always show the detail strip; do not shrink labels to fit.
- **States.** Raise visited/revealed/unexplored fills to at least 3:1 against the base; for example visited
  `#50725a`, revealed `#354f40`, unexplored `#23372b` with a dashed border. Use a gold outline plus an arrow/
  “YOU ARE HERE” tag for the current room; use distinct icons for door, beacon, NPC, sigil and unexplored
  rooms. Add patterns or icons, not colour alone.
- **Map controls.** Default to fit-to-panel. On phone, allow a 1.3–1.6× zoom and pan (wheel/pinch/drag), plus a
  “fit” button; keep room hit targets at least 24×24 CSS px. Mark off-screen points of interest with edge
  arrows. The MDPI study and minimap guidance both stress that too-small cartography becomes decorative and
  that high-saturation markers should sit over a low-saturation base [23][24][25].
- **Accessible equivalent.** Give the canvas `role="img"` and a concise `aria-label` (“Map of Hollowmere.
  Current room: Mossgate Threshold. 1 of 12 rooms visited.”), and include a visually hidden list of rooms with
  visited/revealed state. A canvas alone is invisible to screen readers.
- **Layout.** Let the map use 45–55% of the available journal height on phone, then the detail/legend; avoid the
  current 430 px minimum panel with 300 px of empty space. The legend should fit on two lines at 390 px; use
  8–9 px legend text only because it is supplementary, and keep 3:1 swatches.
- **Acceptance.** No clipped words; all room labels ≥11 px; all map states ≥3:1; current room unmistakable;
  labels/detail available at 390×844; keyboard and screen reader can enumerate rooms.

### Tabs, lists and CTA hierarchy

The captured tab row is 10 px text, 29–36 px tall; on phone it is 8 px and ~24–32 px tall. The footer continues
to advertise `↑ ↓ SELECT`, `ENTER USE/TOGGLE`, `1–5 DIRECT` on a touch viewport. The mobile note says the
journal is “read-only” while the lantern holds, but inventory and options are interactive. Fix the copy, not
just the layout.

- Use the WAI-ARIA tabs pattern: `role="tablist"` / `role="tab"` / `role="tabpanel"`, `aria-selected`,
  `aria-controls`, roving `tabindex`, Left/Right/Home/End, focus visible, and a focusable panel if it has no
  focusable child [4]. The current global key handler can remain as a shortcut layer, but it must not be the
  only semantics.
- Make the journal a real dialog: `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="true"`, label/describe it, trap focus, put
  initial focus on the selected tab, and restore focus on close. Hide the HUD from assistive tech while open.
- Inventory rows should be buttons or a listbox with an explicit **Use** action. First tap selects; a second
  tap or the Use button consumes. Show `rarity`, `usable`, effect preview and a disabled reason; the state
  already exposes both fields but the UI ignores them. Cinder's `CONSUMABLE · ENTER USE` line and leading
  selection mark are the right pattern; borrow that clarity. Tooltips can repeat that information on hover or
  focus, but must never be the only place it exists. A persistent selected-item detail pane is safer on touch
  and easier to read than a transient tooltip.
- Options should be grouped (“Comfort”, “Presentation”, “Input”) with a one-line description per row and a
  proper switch (`role="switch"`, `aria-checked`) or button. ON/OFF text alone is weak and the row is 41 px.
- Use a 4/8/12/16/24/32 spacing scale, one primary CTA per panel, and 44 px touch rows. Current tabs at 32–38 px
  fail the enhanced 44 px target and are borderline for the 24 px minimum when labels wrap [3].
- Empty states should point to the next action, not just say “The satchel is empty.” Add a “Next thread” card
  (“Bind the Mossgate Beacon”, “Find the Sun Sigil in the Rootways”) and show sealed capabilities at 0.6–0.7
  opacity with a location hint, not 0.42 opacity plus grayscale.

Typography inside the journal should be: item names 14–15 px, descriptions 12–13 px in the serif face, metadata
10 px mono, headings 20–22 px. The current 8–8.5 px mono descriptions are the main readability failure. Keep
line length around 45–75 characters; the 1040 px panel currently lets descriptions run far wider than useful
[10][11].

### Generic panel and spacing

The panel is a single large card with a small cluster of content at the top. It reads as an empty container.
Use a two-column layout on wide screens (map + detail; stats + milestones; options + help) and a single column
on phone. Give each panel a visible subhead and a “what to do next” row. A content-height panel is better than
a fixed 380–430 px minimum, but the content still needs a layout that fills the available height intentionally.

## 5. Palette, lighting, terrain and depth

### Findings

- The floor is a single 64 px grass texture repeated at ~47 px per tile across 18 rows. Every tile has the same
  internal pattern and the same grid seam, producing a checkerboard that competes with characters. The motion
  version of the repeated-pattern guideline is a useful stress test for the play band [6].
- The background is one image drawn with a `max(width, height)` cover fit and a flat 58% black overlay. Its
  parallax is only `cameraX * 2`, with no separate midground or foreground.
- Props are drawn with primitive rectangles/arcs: the green banner is a translucent vertical bar, the arch is a
  thin semicircle, the beacon is a pole plus a large flat teal disc, and the lore pickup is a large gold disc.
  These read as placeholder debug shapes rather than objects in the world.
- The world palette is pleasing but low-contrast in the play band: the goblin's grey-green silhouette and the
  brown NPC both blend into the grass/tree values. Interactables (beacon and pickup) use cool glows while the
  HUD uses gold and teal; the semantic colour language is not consistent.

### Recommendation

**Depth bands.** Use three or four distinct rates: back 0.15, mid 0.5, play 1.0, foreground 1.12. Do not use
eight near-identical rates. The composition guidance is consistent: back layer carries value and atmosphere,
midground carries place identity, play band stays quiet, foreground frames the edges and never crosses the
centre [20][21][22]. A foreground layer at 1.12–1.15 is enough; 1.6 reads as an assault.

**Terrain.** Build 4–6 deterministic variants of each 64 px tile (flip, 90° rotation, crop, edge overlay), select
with a stable `(x*17 + y*29 + roomSeed)` hash, and add decals (grass tufts, stones, roots, cracks) at sparse
seeded positions. The texture is displayed near its native 64 px scale; higher-resolution source art will not
fix the checkerboard, so spend the effort on variation and edge treatment. Add a 2–4 px contact/edge highlight
where a floor tile meets empty space and a darker ambient-occlusion strip where floor meets wall. Pre-render the
static floor/wall layer into an offscreen canvas per room; draw it as one image per frame. This removes the
per-tile grid and makes the repetition far less visible without new art.

**Lighting.** Establish one key direction (the background art reads as light from the upper left/centre) and
keep rim highlights consistent. Use a warm key and cool shadow, local pools for torches/beacon with radial
gradients rather than flat discs, and a slow mist band between midground and play band. Replace the flat 58%
scrim with atmospheric perspective: desaturate distant layers by 20–40%, compress their value range, and shift
them toward the sky/haze colour; keep the play band at full contrast [21][22]. The current haze gradient at the
bottom of the playfield is a good start, but it dims the characters too; move it behind entities where possible.

**Palette discipline.** Limit each region to 5–7 values. Reserve saturated gold for loot/interaction and warm
red-orange for danger; health and focus keep their green/teal identity in the HUD. Run the scene in grayscale:
the player, enemies, hazards and interactables must remain distinct from the background. Add a 1 px warm rim
light or contact shadow to enemies/NPCs. The player and Brann currently stand shoulder to shoulder in the
dialogue capture with similar value and silhouette; a rim light plus a small name/interaction marker would
separate them.

**Props.** Replace the banner rectangle with a folded cloth silhouette and two attachment points; give the arch
two depth columns; make the beacon a lantern with a warm, soft pool rather than a teal disc; reduce pickup glow
radius and add a small outline/sparkle. These are cheap canvas primitives but should read as objects, not UI
rectangles. Background region art already exists in `content.json`; use the current region's background in the
journal shell instead of hard-coding `hollow-bg-forest.png` in CSS.

**HUD contrast over the world.** The top-left HUD sits over different values in each region. Add a local top-edge
gradient scrim (not a full panel) behind the brand/status and behind the room header; test against the brightest
archive frame. The captured text has a heavy shadow, but shadow is not a substitute for a stable background.
Text should meet 4.5:1; bars and sigil states are meaningful graphics and need 3:1 [1][2].

## 6. Accessibility and preferences

### Must-have settings

Restructure the Options tab into:

- **Comfort:** Reduced motion (off/on, default follows `prefers-reduced-motion`), Screen shake (off/low/full,
  or a 0–100% slider), Reduce flashes, Damage numbers.
- **Display:** HUD scale (Compact/Standard/Large), High contrast, Background dim.
- **Input:** Control hints, remapping later; menu hints adapt to keyboard/touch/gamepad.

Each row needs a one-line description. The current four labels (“Screen shake”, “Damage numbers”, “Control
hints”, “Reduce flashes”) do not explain what “Reduce flashes” covers. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for
canvas effects, not just CSS transitions [32]. Reduced motion should suppress screen shake, or make that an
explicit exception. “Reduce flashes” must not remove the low-health warning entirely; replace the pulse with
a static red edge and “LOW” label. Xbox XAG 117 and the Game Accessibility Guidelines both expect camera
motion/shake to be adjustable or removable [5][7].

### Semantics and input

- Tabs and dialog as described above; health/focus/boss bars as `role="progressbar"` with live values.
- `aria-live="polite"` for room discovery, save confirmation, item use, boss phase and objective changes.
  Do not announce every damage number.
- Focus-visible styles on tabs, rows, options and dialogue choices (some are present; extend to all interactive
  rows). Use a real `<button>` for every clickable row so Enter/Space work natively.
- Keep 24×24 CSS px as the absolute minimum and 44×44 for touch; the current 32–38 px tabs and 41 px options
  are too small for comfortable phone use [3].
- Do not rely on colour alone: sigils need filled/unfilled shapes, damage needs signs/glyphs, map states need
  patterns/icons, and low health needs text [33].
- Ensure text can scale to 200% without clipping; current fixed px sizes and narrow rows will break. Test at
  200% browser zoom and with a large system font.
- On touch, either implement movement/jump/attack controls or state that a keyboard is required. A phone-sized
  journal with no way to leave the menu and play is a demo, not mobile support.

## 7. Performance budgets and implementation

The frame budget for 60 Hz is 16.7 ms total; leave roughly 6–8 ms for the browser and aim for under ~8 ms of
application JS per frame at 1280×800, with 120 Hz devices at 8.3 ms. Measure P95 frame time, not average FPS
[29][30].

### Concrete performance fixes

- **Cache static room geometry.** Build the `#`/`=` tiles, room props and door frames into an offscreen canvas
  keyed by room id + room revision. Draw one image per frame instead of ~500 `drawImage` calls. Redraw only when
  the room changes or a door/checkpoint state changes. Pre-rendering repeated primitives is the standard canvas
  optimization [27][28].
- **Stop per-frame DOM work.** `renderDialogue` currently replaces its children every frame while a dialogue is
  open; rebuild only when the dialogue signature/revision changes. `drawHud` recreates the three sigil spans
  every frame; update classes/text only when values change. Cache the last HUD key.
- **Use compositor-friendly bar updates.** Animate `transform: scaleX()` on the fill (origin left) rather than
  transitioning `width`, which triggers layout. Only write when the numeric value changes.
- **Avoid canvas resets and shadow blur.** `drawMap` sets `mapCanvas.width/height` every draw; resize only when
  the measured size changes. Per-object `shadowBlur` is expensive; pre-render projectile/slash glows as small
  offscreen sprites or use radial gradients once. Cap particles (the current `effects.ts` limits of 12 labels,
  16 impacts and 60 particles are sensible).
- **Pause rendering behind the journal.** When `status === 'paused'`, stop redrawing the live world; use the
  last frame or a static blurred snapshot as the journal background. The current frame loop keeps rendering
  the world under a full-screen opaque journal.
- **Resize correctly.** Use `ResizeObserver` on the canvas/root in addition to `window.resize`, and re-render
  the map when its CSS size changes. A phone rotation while the journal is open must not leave a stale
  low-resolution canvas.
- **Draw less.** Cull effects and sprites outside the view, pre-render tile variants and backgrounds, round
  draw coordinates to integers where appropriate, and keep DPR capped at 2 (already done).
- **Budget test.** Capture at 1280×800 and 390×844 with 4× CPU throttling; assert no long task >50 ms during
  room entry or journal opening. Use Playwright's performance metrics or a tiny in-page frame-time sampler in
  the demo script.

The current demo only captures start, dialogue and journal; it does not exercise combat. Add a combat scenario
that attacks the Mossgate goblin and captures at least one frame with an active damage label and one with a
telegraph. Unit-test the pure feedback helpers (`effects.ts`) for caps, culling, label size and reduced-motion
behaviour.

## 8. Cinder comparison: what to borrow, what not to

Cinder's read-only screens get some things right that Hollowmere should copy:

| Cinder pattern | Why it works | Hollowmere use |
|---|---|---|
| Health as a 42 px numeral with a 7 px bar | The value is the anchor; the bar is secondary | Adopt the idea, scale to 28–32 px |
| Selected pack row has a leading `›`, a type line and `ENTER USE/EQUIP` | The action is unambiguous | Add explicit Use and item type/rarity |
| Class cards have a large title, readable body and keycap | Choice is scannable | Use a similar title/body/keypad hierarchy in dialogue and capabilities |
| Bottom-corner health / ammo clusters | HUD stays out of the centre | Keep Hollowmere's corners, but reduce chrome |

Do not copy Cinder's 8–10 px metadata or its dark, low-contrast automap; both products share the same microtype
bias. Hollowmere's serif/gold journal is the stronger identity. Keep it, but let the player-facing copy breathe
at 12–16 px.

The Composer screenshots are a developer tool, not player UI. Its 10 px body and 7 px metadata are acceptable
for dense authoring, but the same ramp should not leak into the game journal. A separate pass can improve
validation-message contrast and focus rings if the tool becomes user-facing.

## 9. Suggested implementation order

1. **Lock the screen-space feedback contract** (labels, slash, projectiles, rings). Reuse the screen-space
   `effects.ts` helpers; add merge/anchoring and reduced-motion. Add unit tests for caps and projection.
2. **Retune the HUD ramp and mobile reflow.** Health number first; reduce brand/room emphasis; add safe-area
   and 390×844/844×390 layout checks.
3. **Fix the map and journal semantics.** Label measurement/ellipsis, state contrast, selected-room detail,
   ARIA tabs/dialog, touch targets, explicit Use. This is the largest player-facing readability win after
   combat.
4. **Add feedback tiers.** Target flash, impact ring/sparks, directional shake, i-frame rim, telegraph
   progress. Add hitstop only with a small game-state contract; otherwise correct the docs.
5. **Depth pass.** Pre-render terrain, add variants/decals, parallax bands, foreground framing, lighting pools
   and prop silhouettes.
6. **Accessibility and performance pass.** Reduced motion, flash gating, progressbar/live-region semantics,
   DOM caching, room-layer caching, frame-time tests.

## 10. Acceptance checklist

**Combat**

- [ ] Damage labels are 12–16 CSS px, capped at 12, never scale with the camera, and never overlap or sit under the HUD.
- [ ] A three-hit combo reads as one running total within 0.6 s.
- [ ] Hit, heavy/kill, player hurt and heal are visually distinct in grayscale.
- [ ] If hitstop ships: 40–70 ms normal / 70–110 ms heavy, ≤120 ms, one active stop, input buffered.
- [ ] Shake is ≤6 px desktop / ≤4 px phone, directional, 150–250 ms, only meaningful impacts, and can be off.
- [ ] Player i-frames are visible without hiding the player; telegraph lead is ≥350 ms.

**HUD and journal**

- [ ] No player-facing text below 11 px desktop / 12 px phone (supplementary mono labels may be 9–10 px).
- [ ] Health is the primary visual anchor; low health has a non-colour cue.
- [ ] At 390×844 and 844×390, brand/room/currency/health/focus do not overlap or leave safe areas.
- [ ] Map labels are unclipped or ellipsized with a full-name detail; all map states are ≥3:1; current room is unmistakable.
- [ ] Tabs follow the ARIA tabs pattern; the journal traps/restores focus; every clickable row is a button; item use is explicit.
- [ ] Phone shows touch-appropriate hints or clearly states keyboard required.

**World**

- [ ] No visible repeated tile pattern across the play band at normal camera distance.
- [ ] At least three depth rates with atmospheric perspective; foreground never crosses the centre.
- [ ] Player, enemies, hazards and interactables remain distinct in grayscale and over the brightest region.
- [ ] Region background is used in the journal, not hard-coded forest art.

**Comfort and performance**

- [ ] `prefers-reduced-motion` disables shake, float/pop, motes, pulses and blink; explicit options override it.
- [ ] Reduce-flash replaces rather than removes low-health/telegraph information; full-screen flashes ≤3/s.
- [ ] Text scales to 200% without clipping; focus indicators are visible; touch targets ≥24 px, 44 px preferred.
- [ ] 60 Hz build stays under ~8 ms JS/frame at 1280×800; no per-frame `replaceChildren`; static room layer cached; map redraws on resize.
