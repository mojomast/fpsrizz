# Acceptance strategy: three future games

Research note, 2026-11-16. The dungeon crawler, RPG and household life simulator are not yet designed. The gates below are proposed requirements for their eventual designs, not claims that any game or test already exists. They deliberately allow different mechanics, camera styles, control schemes and save policies. Each game needs its own evidence; a passing shared engine test is not a substitute for a playable game route.

## Definition of automated completion

Before implementation is considered complete, each design must publish a **scope manifest** with stable IDs and counts for all shipped regions/levels or household spaces, quests/objectives, actors, interactions, items, enemy or challenge types, choices, outcomes, and supported controls as applicable. Mark optional and mutually exclusive content and any intentionally unreachable decorative content. Define starting state, legal actions, success and failure/recovery conditions, game time units, seed behavior, and whether persistence is supported. For an open-ended simulator, define at least two concrete milestone outcomes with observable state; an endless mode alone is not evidence of multiple outcomes. Explicitly list any intentionally unsupported category rather than treating it as untested.

The release evidence for **each** game must include:

1. The manifest linked to actual bundled preset/content IDs, a content-to-test matrix with no unaccounted shipped entries, and passing validation and coverage checks described below. Counts are set by design, not arbitrary numbers in this note; a single room or superficial variation does not establish a substantial game. Document why the scope is substantial in terms of distinct mechanics, locations and authored progress.
2. At least **two mutually exclusive or meaningfully divergent complete routes** from a fresh start, each using legal player actions and reaching a distinct, asserted ending or milestone outcome. Divergence must alter authoritative world, relationship, progression, or outcome state, not just dialogue text or score. Record the decisive choice, prerequisites, consequences and terminal/milestone assertions. Include a failed or blocked action and a defined loss or recovery route where the design supports one. If no loss exists, demonstrate a meaningful setback and recovery instead.
3. Headless tests of every documented command family and its rejection/edge cases, plus browser tests using actual advertised controls for at least one representative full core loop and the decisive action of each route. The complete routes may use public command ingress in headless tests; do not claim browser end-to-end completion unless the browser actually drives the whole route. Verify effects against authoritative read-only state and visible UI; direct state mutation, teleportation and debug command invocation do not count as player-input coverage.
4. Deterministic replay, lifecycle, persistence if supported, UI, substitution where applicable, and regression gates below all pass. Run the repository's required checks and record commands, versions, seed, route IDs, outputs, screenshots on failure, and any environmental failures. An unavailable browser is a blocked browser gate, not a pass.

Automation establishes the above objective completeness. Human evaluation of pacing, balance, readability, aesthetics, enjoyment and narrative feel is valuable separately, but subjective playtesting is **not** a prerequisite for calling the automated scope complete. Required manual audio or pointer-lock verification under `AGENTS.md`, when those areas change, remains a separate platform check; report it independently.

## Content and route coverage

Validate all bundled content through real staging and the content `Reporter`: unique IDs, resolvable references, ranges, missing assets/theme keys, role selection, valid start states and actionable path-bearing errors for deliberately broken fixtures. Enumerate the manifest against actual loaded content so that adding an unlisted entry fails the coverage check. Exercise every authored quest/objective, interaction and named outcome in a reachable route or explicitly identified alternate-start fixture; ensure all mutually exclusive branches are covered across fresh sessions. Static reachability of a room graph is useful but insufficient: prerequisites, resources, timing and actual transitions must permit the route. Use bounded route drivers with a clear timeout and per-step state assertions; never turn an unreachable route into a pass by changing its state directly.

| Game | Minimum concrete route and content evidence, to adapt once designed |
|---|---|
| Dungeon crawler | Traverse every authored floor/region and access-gate at least once across routes; exercise each enemy/challenge type, loot/item effect and boss or final challenge. One complete route obtains and uses a gate-enabling item or equivalent; another chooses a materially different path, build or resolution with a distinct resulting outcome. Test blocked locked passage, insufficient resources, combat/hazard failure and recovery or restart, and a reward awarded once. Assert map/position, inventory, health and final result at key steps. |
| RPG | Cover each authored quest and significant dialogue choice across runs, including prerequisites, mutually exclusive resolutions, reward/flag consequences and downstream reactions. Two fresh-start routes make conflicting consequential choices and reach distinct endings or milestone states; assert quest journal, party/relationships, inventory/progression and outcome. Exercise at least two designed character-development or problem-solving approaches, with invalid equipment/skill/quest actions unable to spend or advance state. |
| Household life simulator | Exercise every shipped household activity, need/resource, object class, social interaction and time transition across runs; assert costs, effects, scheduling and bounded values. Two routes make incompatible household decisions and reach separately defined milestone outcomes (for example relationship versus career priorities, if actually designed), with observable changed relationships/resources/world state. Include unmet need or affordability rejection, a schedule conflict and a setback/recovery across a day boundary. Verify autonomous behavior against the documented pause and time rules. |

For each command family, test accepted action, invalid/malformed or unknown target, insufficient prerequisite/cost, out-of-range target, repeated command and terminal/paused ingress as applicable. Assert no unintended partial cost, clock advance, objective update or duplicate event on rejection, according to the documented policy. If queued, cover bounded capacity, ordering, stale targets and whether acceptance means enqueue rather than execution. Test legality before committing shared actions; assertions should use independently specified expected outcomes, not merely duplicate implementation calculations.

## Seeded simulation and isolation

Use the real host and fixed scheduler, as in `tests/helpers/game.ts`. For each game, record a seed, initial content/preset, explicit tick-indexed input/command tape and checkpoint snapshots. Run the same tape in two fresh hosts and after restart; compare ordered gameplay-relevant events and canonical authoritative state at each checkpoint and completion. Exclude only explicitly identified cosmetic/render fields and document any numeric tolerance. Include a random-dependent encounter or decision if randomness is part of the design; use at least two fixed seeds and assert a known seed-dependent difference for a fixture chosen to produce one, rather than assuming any two seeds must differ. For deterministic nonrandom designs, assert equivalent fixed-tick behavior and state that seed variation is inapplicable.

Interleave two hosts with the same IDs and seeds, advance and pause them independently, mutate one through public inputs, then stop/restart it; the other host's state and events must remain unchanged. Review that simulation uses `ctx.rng` and `ctx.time.now`, not wall-clock or cosmetic RNG, and has no mutable plugin module state. Replay claims cover the recorded fixed-tick tapes and specified observable state, not arbitrary frame rates or unrecorded input timing.

## Ownership, restart and save data

- Start, perform meaningful actions, stop and restart repeatedly; compare host resource reports and browser UI roots, scene roots, owned styles and render allocations against an appropriate per-session baseline. Verify no duplicate systems/listeners/events or growing ownership counts, fresh transient state, and intended persistent state only. After teardown, retained handles and queued work must not mutate a new session; late async completions must be guarded. Exercise both consumer and provider disposal for any owned shared instances and verify independent hosts remain alive.
- Exercise staging of invalid content/role selection while a game runs: report a useful error and verify the old session still ticks. Separately test activation failure after commit without assuming the old session is restored, matching host lifecycle semantics. Verify missing selected providers fail rather than silently falling back.
- If saving is supported, specify version, keys, save points, what is and is not restored, migration policy and observable error/recovery behavior. Save after a consequential branch and noninitial inventory/relationships/time, recreate host and storage adapter, load, and assert state plus continuation to the correct outcome. Check restart versus new game versus reload semantics and isolation of unrelated profile keys.
- Test absent save, malformed JSON, wrong types, unknown IDs, missing fields, out-of-range/nonfinite values, unsupported older/newer versions, truncated data and incompatible game/content IDs. Assert documented rejection or migration, no partial application, no crash, and preservation of the original bytes unless the documented explicit recovery action replaces them. Test unavailable storage, read/write/remove errors and quota failure with no false success; test pause/save timing if relevant. `tests/profile-store.test.ts` covers the outer profile store, so game-specific nested save validation still needs its own cases. If no persistence is offered, declare that explicitly and test that restart/new session behavior matches the documented nonpersistent policy.

## Browser and presentation gates

Build `dist/` and run real Chrome/Playwright as the existing scripts do. For each game test desktop 1280×800 and narrow 390×844, using advertised keyboard and pointer/touch-accessible UI controls as supported. Start from launcher, perform a movement or selection, interaction, meaningful game action and decisive branch action, pause/resume and restart; assert corresponding visible feedback and authoritative state. Use DOM keyboard/click events, focus and accessibility roles/labels; do not use `__bulkhead` to submit actions. Its test-only handle may read state and reports, as in `tests/portfolio-qol-demo/run.mjs`. Verify that an action during shell pause does not commit or replay on resume when the ingress policy forbids it.

At both sizes, check core controls and relevant dialogs are visible, enabled when legal, keyboard reachable and not obscured/clipped; test scroll access, focus retention/return after overlays, and no unintended horizontal document overflow. Check responsive HUD, readable status/feedback, ending/milestone and error states; capture screenshots for diagnosis, with explicit DOM/geometry assertions rather than screenshot existence as the oracle. Assert no unexpected console/page errors, visible canvas or world where relevant, and no accumulating UI/render roots after repeated restarts. A smoke test using test-mode pointer-lock bypass does not prove real pointer lock or audio behavior.

## Regression and provider substitution

Run `npm run check` (typecheck, import boundaries, reuse-index freshness and all node tests), `npm run validate-game -- --bundled`, `npm run build && npm run smoke`, and the new game's browser acceptance scripts. Run the creator demo if creator UI changes. Regenerate the reuse index after source/catalog/metadata changes and retain existing tests and lint rules. Record failures and environment limitations rather than skipping them. These commands are future implementation gates; this note itself does not assert they passed.

For every shared role actually consumed, run its existing conformance cases and a representative game route through the production selection. Where an independent compatible alternate exists, select it via the role in a catalog that excludes the default provider; replay the same route and compare authoritative outcomes and relevant ordered receipts, plus rejection, pause and teardown. `tests/navigation-consumers.test.ts` demonstrates default-absent selection; `docs/mechanic-conformance.md` documents resource conformance and the precise limits of existing substitution evidence. Existing alternate navigation, progression, resources and sequence helpers are candidates only when their contracts fit the actual game. If no independent alternative exists, record the gap and do not claim substitution; a mock delegating to the default is not independent evidence. Provider-specific allocation counts need not match, but cleanup must return to each provider's own baseline.

## Existing patterns and evidence boundaries

- `tests/hollowmere.test.ts`: fixed-tick real-input movement, public commands, graph checks and checkpoint fixtures; graph reachability alone does not establish all legal paths.
- `tests/portfolio-resource-games.test.ts`: state-reactive complete routes, rejection, events and restart; direct command tests are not browser-control evidence.
- `tests/host-lifecycle.test.ts` and `tests/helpers/game.ts`: real host lifecycle and headless fake platform services; test registration survives restart while activation is fresh.
- `scripts/smoke.mjs` drives actual keyboard events and records browser resources but also uses debug fast-forward and teleportation for some checks. `tests/portfolio-qol-demo/run.mjs` uses read-only service state and UI actions at desktop and narrow sizes. Browser scripts are separate from `npm test` and need Playwright and Chrome.
- `tests/profile-store.test.ts` establishes outer corrupt/incompatible storage behavior; individual games must prove their own save schema and continuation. `AGENTS.md` sets scoped ownership, deterministic simulation, content reporting and required checks; boundary lint alone cannot prove those invariants.
